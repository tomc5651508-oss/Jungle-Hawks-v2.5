Jungle Hawks is the first Pacific overhaul built natively into IL-2 Great Battles. Not a skin pack, not a mission
pack: a complete theatre -
new aircraft, two dynamic careers on two new maps, carriers that sail with the fleet, reworked flight models, a new
AI, new fire and damage effects and a heavier soundscape. Every part sits inside the game's own systems - the hangar,
the career, quick missions, the mission editor and multiplayer - instead of beside them. Over 10 GB of it, built by one
person over a year and a half, as the Pacific campaign Great Battles never had.

**At a glance**

- **19 Pacific aircraft** - Japanese Navy and Army, US Navy, USAAF and RAAF - flying in careers, quick missions and the
  editor like stock planes
- **Two dynamic careers** - New Guinea and Guadalcanal / the Solomons, 1942-43, with a front line that moves by date
- **Experimental: carrier task forces** - Shokaku and Enterprise with escorts, deck take-offs and landings,
  aerial torpedoes: planned features with test assets and designs - play them and send feedback
- **Flight models** refitted per type to the real aircraft's weight, wing area, drag and power
- **Better BoX AI** - flights that fight as leaders and wingmen, break off, regroup and go home
- **Impact FX** - staged fires, calibre-scaled hits, smoke you can see from the next valley
- **Sound** - heavy calibre-scaled guns and VSM engines on every aircraft
- **Two new maps** - New Guinea and Guadalcanal, with jungle strips, native villages, harbours and the Kokoda Track
- **Custom ground and naval assets** - Daihatsu barges, Ha-Go tanks, M3 Stuarts, destroyers, transports, carriers
- **Japanese career depth** - period pilot portraits, unit emblems and Japanese tac codes
- **Multiplayer** - a mod-matched handshake for players and a dedicated-server kit

## The aircraft

Nineteen types, each integrated natively on a Great Battles airframe: remastered geometry and materials, its own
cockpit view and sights, real loadouts, seated crews, redrawn skins and period markings. They appear in the hangar,
careers, quick missions and the mission editor exactly like the stock aircraft.

## Careers

Two dynamic careers on the game's own career engine - real squadrons, real airfields, a front line that moves by date.
Pick a side, join a squadron and fly the campaign as it happened.

- **New Guinea, 1942-43**: Port Moresby, Milne Bay, Kokoda, Buna-Gona and Goodenough. 39 dated front-line snapshots
  keep the fighting where it happened: infantry and supply columns on the track, AA only at defended bases, Ha-Go tanks
  at Milne Bay, M3 Stuarts at Buna, Daihatsu barges on the coast.
- **Guadalcanal and the Solomons, to September 1943**: Henderson Field, Tulagi, Lunga Roads and the islands up the Slot.
- **Squadrons**: RAAF, RNZAF and RAF (a Commonwealth side with its own voices), USAAF, US Navy, USMC, and the Japanese
  Navy and Army air forces, each with its own emblem. Japanese pilots get period portraits, not German ones.
- **Missions that get to the point**: air starts about 14 km short of the objective and a mix of short sorties, so
  more of your evening is spent fighting than transiting.

## AI wingmen that fly as a flight

Better BoX AI replaces the stock AI's lone-wolf habits with flight discipline.

- **Leaders and wingmen**: flights fly as pairs and sections. A wingman covers the shooter, takes the hand-off when the
  leader breaks, and rejoins when he straggles.
- **Attacks by doctrine**: patrol, spread, set up, commit, with timing that differs between the Japanese and Allied
  sides. Fighters use the sun.
- **Each type fights its own way**: turners turn, energy fighters dive and extend, bombers avoid fighters.
- **Gunnery by skill**: aim error, burst length and fire discipline follow the pilot's skill.
- **They break off for real**: a flight that disengages holds its egress for 150 s instead of turning back into the
  fight a second later. Out of ammunition means going home.
- **Readable markers**: friend and foe marker colours and aircraft type names on the in-flight labels.

## Skins and tactical codes

Each aircraft has its own paint schemes in the hangar, with the default first. Tactical codes are set in the game's

for the 251st and more than 20 others). Army aircraft get a three-digit number on the rear fuselage. US and RAAF
aircraft use their own national styles.

## Flight models

Every Jungle Hawks aircraft is flown on a stock Great Battles airframe - and then rebuilt until it flies like the real
one. Not a renamed Bf 109 with a Zero painted on: weight, wing, drag, engine and the AI's own tables are refitted per
type, then checked against the published wartime figures.

**What was refitted, per aircraft**

- **Weight** - the host flies at the real loaded weight; part masses and inertia are scaled to match
- **Wing** - lift refitted to the real wing area, so wing loading (and with it turn and stall) is the real aircraft's
- **Drag** - refitted so the top speed lands at the right height
- **Engine** - take-off power and the full power-with-height curve, supercharger gears included (single-speed Sakae 12,
  two-speed Sakae 21 and Ha-115, the R-2800, the Hercules)
- **AI** - the AI performance tables are refitted too, so the AI flies the same aircraft you do

**Checked like the real thing.** Each type has its own two-page technical note: maximum speed against height, climb and
time to height, power against height, specific excess power, a sea-level turn (E-M) diagram and best turn time against
wing loading - with the historical figures marked on every chart and the Bf 109 F-4 drawn in for scale. The Japanese
types are bound as a 1943 RAAF intelligence handbook, the Allied types as a 1943 IJN technical arsenal one:

- [RAAF Handbook of Japanese Aircraft Performance Charts](docs/fm/RAAF_Handbook_Japanese_Aircraft_Performance.pdf) -
  11 types, 66 figures
- [IJN Handbook of Allied Aircraft Performance Charts](docs/fm/IJN_Handbook_Allied_Aircraft_Performance.pdf) -
  7 types, 42 figures

## Install

**The installer is the only download.** Get `JH-Installer-<version>.zip` from the [Releases](../../releases) page,
unzip it anywhere and run `Start JH Installer.bat` ([Python 3.10+](https://www.python.org/downloads/) must be
installed). It fetches everything else itself.

1. **Install tab:** tick the parts you want. **Pingu's Jungle Hawks v2.5** is the mod; the three standalones
   (Impact FX, Better BoX AI, Heavy Gun Sounds) are for the stock game, for when Jungle Hawks is off. Press
   **Install / Update** - a download window pops out with a progress bar and log, and the mod lands in
   `<IL-2>\MODS` (created if missing).
2. **Settings tab:** your IL-2 folder (found on Steam automatically), **Enable modes = 1** (IL-2 needs it for mods;
   `data\startup.cfg` is backed up first) and **Desktop shortcut** for the launcher.
3. **Play tab:** back up `data\Career\cp.db`, then press **PLAY JUNGLE HAWKS**. The launcher mounts the mod, starts
   IL-2 and puts the game's own files back when IL-2 closes. **Unload Jungle Hawks** does the same by hand.
4. **Aircraft Info:** every aircraft's data sheet, the stock aircraft it replaces, and the flight model handbooks.

The launcher uses the same `MODS` folder as JSGME, with JSGME's own logs and backups, so JSGME works too - enable or
disable Pingu's Jungle Hawks v2.5 in either. JSGME itself is optional.

The installer checks for updates when it starts and never updates by itself: a newer release shows
**Installed - Update avail**, and you press Install / Update when you want it.

### Multiplayer

Every player and the server need the same build. The bundled GB Mod Loader checks this when you join and refuses
mismatched installs.
